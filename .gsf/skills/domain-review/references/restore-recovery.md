# Backup and actual restore proof

Use with [domain review](../SKILL.md). Operational execution belongs to an authorized operator under the worker contract, not a read-only reviewer. Coordinate recovery planning with [incident response](../../incident-response/SKILL.md) and [release](../../release/SKILL.md) when applicable.

## Inputs and objective selection

Identify the product/data owner, affected consumer journeys, data classification, dataset/dependencies, actual engine/storage versions, backup mechanism and schedule, full/incremental/log-chain inventory, retention and deletion policy, encryption key/KMS identity and access, restore tooling and isolated destination. Obtain chosen product **RPO** (maximum acceptable lost committed data) and **RTO** (time from the defined incident/recovery start to usable verified service), their source and who accepts residual loss. Do not invent objectives or infer recoverability from a backup-success flag. Missing necessary backup, key access, isolation or authoritative objectives blocks a recovery claim.

## Sequence and decisions

1. Enumerate failure scenarios: accidental deletion, corruption discovered late, unavailable region/account, lost key access and application/schema incompatibility. Map each to the needed backup/log point and dependencies, including object storage, configuration, identity and secrets restored through approved secret management rather than copied into evidence.
2. Inspect backup completion/integrity metadata and log continuity; choose a recovery point before corruption. Calculate expected RPO from the last recoverable commit relative to the incident cutoff, not simply the schedule. Verify key versions remain available for retained encrypted backups, including access under the intended recovery identity. A checksum proves transfer integrity, not a usable restore.
3. Establish a genuinely separate authorized destination with sufficient storage/compute/version compatibility and restricted access. Confirm exact identity and isolation using actual platform controls. Prevent restored jobs, webhooks, email, payment calls and replicated credentials from affecting real users; absent provable containment blocks execution. Record cost/resource ownership and cleanup boundaries.
4. Perform the actual restore using the selected full/incremental/log chain and approved keys. Record start/end milestones, transfer/replay times, errors and tool/configuration identity without keys or personal data. Validate schema, constraints, record counts, consistency and dependencies; reconcile selected committed transactions before and after the cutoff. Detect silent corruption with application-level invariants, not counts alone.
5. Start the candidate-compatible application in that isolated environment and exercise the consumer workflow, authentication/authorization, data reads and safe writes. Include a deletion/tombstone reconciliation step so a restore cannot silently resurrect data whose deletion obligation remains in force. Record observed RPO and end-to-end RTO, including validation and readiness work, against the selected objectives.
6. Deliver a recovery decision: objective met, objective failed or evidence unavailable; identify a usable recovery point and exact cutover prerequisites. A drill is not permission to redirect production traffic. Cutover needs separate authority, concurrency fencing, reconciliation of writes since the recovery point and consumer communication. Distinguish **rollback** (revert code/config/traffic) from **restore** (recover persisted data); rollback may be impossible or insufficient after destructive data changes.

## Failure, recovery and evidence

On missing/corrupt backup, incomplete logs, unusable keys, restore incompatibility or objective overrun, do not overwrite the original dataset or declare success. Preserve sanitized failure evidence, choose another verified point/chain or repair the documented prerequisite and repeat the affected isolated restore. Record additional potential loss and obtain the owner's decision if objectives cannot be met. Evidence includes backup IDs and immutable manifests, key references/access observations, isolation proof, timestamps, actual restore receipts, integrity/application results and RTO/RPO calculation. Protect evidence access/retention; do not store recovered personal records in the job. Remove only owned drill resources after evidence capture and authorized cleanup; retain the source backups under their policy.

## Consumer acceptance scenario

Restore a selected encrypted backup plus logs to an isolated destination after simulating deletion of a customer order in the owned fixture. With the intended recovery identity, prove a pre-cutoff order is usable and post-cutoff loss is measured; a previously privacy-deleted customer's data must remain inaccessible after tombstone reconciliation. The application must complete its chosen order workflow within the accepted RTO/RPO. A second case with unavailable key access must fail clearly without leaking data or claiming recoverability.
